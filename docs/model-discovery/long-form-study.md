# Long-form musical intelligence — whole-song coherence study (Wave Q, Workstream J, 2026-09-09)

**The question.** The platform's learned note generator, today, is an 8-bar
infiller: Composer's Assistant 2 sees `MAX_LEN` = 1650 tokens at 24 steps per
quarter — a handful of measures of multitrack context — and nothing outside
its window (PR-57/58). The tournament judges 8-bar windows (PR-59). An
arrangement is not a sequence of windows: it opens, states, restates, builds,
peaks, thins and closes, and the parts that carry it come and go for reasons.
Twenty well-judged windows pasted end to end are still twenty windows. This
study asks how the literature gets from local generation to whole-song
coherence, what each route costs, how it fits the platform's *existing*
global-to-local planner stack, and — measured here on real music — what the
data basis for section-level work looks like and how "pasted windows" can be
caught by a metric the tournament can run.

Two kinds of statement appear below and are kept apart: **literature claims**
are cited to their source and are what the papers report about their own
systems; **measurements** are numbers produced here, on the admitted PDMX
corpus, by code in this PR, with the file they are recorded in.

---

## 1. What the platform already has, and what is missing

The planner stack derives, before any note is written: a `GlobalArrangementPlan`
(per-section energy / density / tension targets, climax, motif and contrast
strategy — PR-04, `globalArrangementPlanner.ts`); `SectionPlan`s with function,
active instrument families, roles and 2/4/8-bar `PhrasePlan`s (PR-05,
`sectionPhrasePlanner.ts`); per-window orchestration budgets (PR-06); a
`TransitionPlan` per boundary (PR-08, `transitionEngine.ts`); and, in the V2
request, `motifMemory`, `previousSectionSummary` and `nextSectionIntent`
(PR-42, `partGenerationContextV2.ts`). That is a complete *global-to-local
plan*. In the taxonomy below the platform sits squarely in the "explicit
hierarchical plan, generated top-down" family — with the crucial difference
that its plan is derived by rules from the Song Model rather than sampled by a
model, which is a strength (controllable, explainable, editable) as long as the
note generator actually consumes it.

What is missing is exactly the consumption: the learned generator sees a window
of notes and, in the +CTX arm, has passes applied afterwards. It is not told
"this is the second verse, the chorus is next and bigger, the bass entered last
section, this motif is the song's" in a way it can act on. The corpus side is
missing too: PDMX carries no section labels, so no section-level task exists
(PR-53's tasks are 8-bar part infills, and its honest note said so: *"whole-song
form and long-range development are not in this formulation"*).

## 2. Survey — routes to whole-song coherence

For each approach: what it buys for coherence, what it costs, how it fits a
T5-infill foundation (CA2 first, per the decision report) versus a from-scratch
`ARRANGER_FM`, and how it would consume the platform's plans.

### 2.1 Explicit hierarchical generation (plan first, notes second)

**MusicFrameworks** ([Dai, Jin, Gomes, Dannenberg 2021, arXiv:2109.00663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.00663)).
A full-length melody is organised first by section- and phrase-level structure
(which phrases repeat which), then per phrase a rhythm and a "basic melody" are
generated by separate transformers, then the surface melody is generated
conditioned on basic melody, rhythm and chords. *Buys:* repetition structure
by construction — the repeat is decided at the top, not hoped for at the
bottom. *Costs:* three models, a hand-designed intermediate language, melody
only. *Fit:* the platform's `SectionPlan`/`PhrasePlan` **is** this top level;
what MusicFrameworks adds is the idea that the middle level (a reduced
"skeleton" per phrase) is a generation target of its own.

**MELONS** ([Zou et al. 2021, ICASSP 2022, arXiv:2110.05020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05020))
factors melody generation into *structure generation* (a graph of eight
bar-level relation types: repeat, transposition, rhythm-same, etc.) and
*structure-conditioned melody generation*. *Buys:* a machine-readable form
graph, which is what a coherence metric wants to compare against. *Fit:* the
form string this study extracts from PDMX (`A A B A' C`) is a coarse version
of that graph; MELONS argues for making the graph richer (which bars are
transposed repeats, which share rhythm only).

**Whole-Song Hierarchical Generation with cascaded diffusion** ([Wang, Min, Xia — ICLR 2024, arXiv:2405.09901](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09901)).
Four levels — *form* (phrase arrangement and key, at measure level), *reduced
lead sheet*, *lead sheet*, *accompaniment* — each realised by a diffusion model
conditioned on the levels above, top down, for whole pop songs. The authors
report recognisable global verse–chorus structure and cadences, and higher
quality than baselines. *Buys:* the strongest published evidence that
whole-song form comes from generating the form *as data* and conditioning
everything below on it. *Costs:* four models, piano-roll (image-like)
representations, pop lead-sheet data with phrase labels (POP909-style), no
multitrack orchestration. *Fit (CA2):* not directly — CA2 is autoregressive
token infilling; but the *conditioning discipline* transfers: the platform's
section plan is the "form" level, the Q-04 harmony plan is the "reduced lead
sheet" level, and the generator is the bottom two levels. *Fit (ARRANGER_FM):*
the natural architecture if the platform ever trains from scratch: a form model
is cheap (short sequences), and the note model conditions on it.

**MeloForm** ([Lu et al. 2022, arXiv:2208.14345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.14345))
generates melody with a *given* musical form (e.g. AABA) via an expert system
that expands motifs into phrases and sections, then a neural model that refines
them. *Fit:* closest in spirit to the platform's rules-then-model split; a
useful precedent for "the form is an input, not a sample".

### 2.2 Structure-aware attention inside one long model

**Museformer** ([Yu et al. — NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2210.10349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10349)).
A token in bar *n* attends *finely* to the bars most correlated with musical
structure (the previous 1st, 2nd, 4th, 8th … bars, chosen by similarity
statistics on the corpus) and *coarsely* to one summary token per other bar.
The paper reports modelling sequences over 3× longer than full attention and
better repetition structure. *Buys:* repetition at 4/8/16-bar lags learned
implicitly from the attention pattern; the whole piece is one sequence.
*Costs:* a custom attention kernel; the structural lags are fixed
hyper-parameters (pop-shaped); a decoder-only design. *Fit (CA2):* T5's
encoder is full attention over ≤ 1650 tokens — Museformer's pattern cannot be
retrofitted without retraining the attention; **not compatible** with a
frozen/LoRA CA2. *Fit (ARRANGER_FM):* a strong candidate design for the
note model, especially combined with an explicit plan (the coarse summary
tokens are a natural place to inject a section's plan).

**Compound Word Transformer** ([Hsiao et al. — AAAI 2021, arXiv:2101.02402](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.02402))
groups the tokens of one note (type, beat, pitch, duration, velocity, …) into
one "compound word" predicted with parallel heads, cutting sequence length by
a large constant factor. **PopMAG's MuMIDI** ([Ren et al. — ACM MM 2020, arXiv:2008.07703](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07703))
does the same for *multitrack* accompaniment in one sequence (pitch, duration,
velocity in one step) and adds a long-context memory (Transformer-XL style)
for long-term dependency. **Multitrack Music Transformer** ([Dong et al. — ICASSP 2023, arXiv:2207.06983](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.06983))
represents a note as a six-tuple (type, beat, position, pitch, duration,
instrument) so that many instruments fit a short sequence, with substantial
speed and memory gains at comparable quality. *Buys:* more bars per context at
the same attention budget — the cheapest lever on "how much of the song does
the model see". *Costs:* multi-head output, a bespoke tokenizer, and — for the
platform — a break with CA2's vocabulary. *Fit (CA2):* no; CA2's tokenizer is
fixed. *Fit (ARRANGER_FM):* `ARRANGER_REMI` (386 tokens, 12 steps/quarter,
`arrangerRemi.ts`) is a flat REMI; a compound variant would be the first thing
to change if context length becomes the bottleneck (a 4-token note → 1
position ≈ 4× more bars per window).

**SymphonyNet** ([Liu et al. — ISMIR 2022, arXiv:2205.05448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05448))
models symphonies with a Multi-track Multi-instrument Repeatable representation,
3-D positional embeddings, a *Music BPE* to shorten extra-long token streams,
a linear-attention decoder, and *orchestration as a joint task by masking
instrument information*. *Buys:* a precedent for exactly the platform's
domain — large ensembles over long spans — and for treating "which instrument
plays this" as something the model learns. *Costs:* BPE over music tokens
complicates infilling and control; permutation-invariant training is a
research-grade recipe. *Fit:* from-scratch only.

### 2.3 Long context: extending what one window can see

*Efficient attention* (FlashAttention, [Dao et al. 2022, arXiv:2205.14135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14135))
makes long full attention affordable in wall-clock and memory but does not
change what the model has learned to use. *Position extension* — RoPE
([Su et al., arXiv:2104.09864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09864)), ALiBi
([Press et al., arXiv:2108.12409](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12409)), position
interpolation ([Chen et al. 2023, arXiv:2306.15595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15595)),
YaRN ([Peng et al. 2023, arXiv:2309.00071](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00071)) —
lets a model trained at one length run at a longer one with modest
fine-tuning. *Buys:* more bars of raw context. *Costs:* attention is still
quadratic in the window; and **for T5 specifically**: T5 uses learned
*relative position buckets* (32 buckets, log-spaced, with everything beyond
~128 positions sharing one bucket). Increasing CA2's `MAX_LEN` beyond 1650 is
therefore cheap in code and expensive in *meaning*: the extra positions are
seen through the same far bucket the model already uses, so distance
information degrades exactly where long-range structure lives. A real MAX_LEN
growth is a fine-tune with a new length schedule, not a constant change.
*The honest ceiling:* at 24 steps/quarter and a note ≈ 4–5 tokens, doubling to
3300 tokens buys roughly one more 8-bar window of dense multitrack context.
Long context alone does not reach a whole song; it reaches the neighbouring
window.

**Anticipatory Music Transformer** ([Thickstun, Hall, Donahue, Liang 2023, arXiv:2306.08620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08620))
is the long-context, decoder-only, event-time representation: controls
(fixed events) are interleaved with the generated events ahead of the point
where they will be needed, so infilling is a first-class autoregressive task
over long sequences (trained on Lakh MIDI; human evaluators rated accompaniment
musicality comparable to human over 20-second clips). *Buys:* infilling
without an encoder, over sequences long enough for whole pop songs, with
arbitrary sets of fixed events as controls. *Costs / fit:* Lakh-derived
weights are RESEARCH_ONLY in the registry (PR-54); the *idea* — put the
plan's fixed material and the platform's constraints into the sequence ahead
of time — fits an ARRANGER_FM design.

### 2.4 Memory across windows

**Transformer-XL** ([Dai et al. 2019, arXiv:1901.02860](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.02860))
caches the previous segment's hidden states so the current segment attends to
them; **Compressive Transformer** ([Rae et al. 2019, arXiv:1911.05507](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.05507))
compresses older memories instead of dropping them. **Memorizing Transformers**
([Wu et al. 2022, arXiv:2203.08913](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08913)) add a
kNN lookup over all past keys; the **Recurrent Memory Transformer**
([Bulatov et al. 2022, arXiv:2207.06881](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.06881))
carries a small set of *memory tokens* from segment to segment. In music,
PopMAG used XL-style memory for accompaniment. *Buys:* the model generating
window *k* has a learned summary of windows < *k* — the direct fix for
"pasted windows". *Costs:* training must be segment-sequential (a data-loader
and trainer change); inference is stateful (a worker that keeps memory per
song). *Fit (CA2):* a T5 encoder–decoder has no recurrence; memory tokens
could be *added* as extra encoder inputs (a learned summary of previous
windows produced by a small side model) — this is the one memory idea that
survives a frozen backbone, and it is close to what Section 4 proposes.
*Fit (ARRANGER_FM):* RMT-style memory tokens are cheap to add to a decoder and
train.

### 2.5 Explicit musical memory: motifs, themes, planning tokens

**Theme Transformer** ([Shih, Wu, Zalkow, Müller, Yang 2021, arXiv:2111.04093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.04093))
replaces prompt conditioning with *theme-based conditioning*: the model is
trained to treat the conditioning material as a theme that must manifest
several times in the generation, with themes retrieved from training pieces by
contrastive learning and clustering. *Buys:* restatement as a trained
behaviour rather than a hope. *Fit:* directly relevant to `motifMemory`
(PR-42): the platform already extracts the song's cells; a generator trained
Theme-Transformer-style would be *told* which cells to bring back in which
section. Compatible with CA2 only through the input (put the theme in the
context window as fixed notes); compatible with ARRANGER_FM natively.

**MuseCoco** ([Lu et al. 2023, arXiv:2306.00110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00110))
splits text-to-music into text→attributes and attributes→music, with a fixed
attribute vocabulary (instrumentation, tempo, meter, key, time-signature,
emotion, …) as *prefix tokens*; the paper reports the best musicality and
controllability among its baselines. *Buys:* a proven recipe for attribute
control: **the plan becomes a prefix**. *Fit (CA2):* T5 accepts prefix tokens
in the encoder; a vocabulary-extended fine-tune (decision report, Option B
step ii) is exactly this. *Fit (ARRANGER_FM):* native.

**NotaGen** ([Wang et al. 2025, arXiv:2502.18008](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18008))
is pre-trained on 1.6M ABC pieces, fine-tuned on ~9K classical works
conditioned on period–composer–instrumentation prompts, then optimised with
CLaMP-DPO; its architecture is *hierarchical patches*: a patch-level decoder
over bar-stream patches feeds a character-level decoder. The paper reports
winning A/B tests against baselines. *Buys:* bar-level hierarchy inside one
model (the patch decoder sees the piece at bar granularity, cheaply), and the
DPO recipe with a learned judge. *Costs / fit:* ABC notation, decoder-only,
provenance still LEGAL_REVIEW_REQUIRED (decision report §5); the patch idea is
Museformer's coarse attention in another guise and a good ARRANGER_FM design
input.

**Composer's Assistant 2** ([Malandro 2024, ISMIR, arXiv:2407.14700](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14700)) —
the foundation itself — is a T5-like model for track-measure infilling with
rhythmic conditioning, horizontal/vertical onset-density controls, pitch
controls and a rhythmic-interest control; the paper reports a dramatic
objective improvement over CA1 from those controls. **This is the hook:** CA2
already has *per-measure control tokens*; the platform's section plan is,
per measure, a density target, a register target and an energy target. The
section plan can be spoken to CA2 today through the controls it has, and the
vocabulary-extended fine-tune adds the ones it lacks (section function, role,
chord, motif id).

### 2.6 Section-level latent plans (learned, not rule-derived)

A learned plan (a per-section vector or token sequence sampled by a small
model) is what the cascaded-diffusion and MusicFrameworks lines do; the
platform derives its plan by rules from the Song Model. The two are not in
conflict: a learned *form prior* — "what forms and arcs do human works of this
kind have" — is the natural use of the form profile measured in §3, either to
sanity-check the rule-derived plan (a 40-section plan is not a song) or to
propose forms when the Song Model has none (a vocal-only upload). This study
recommends keeping the plan rule-derived and editable, and using data for a
prior over it, not a replacement.

## 3. Forms the arranger must know

The owner's list — intro, verse, pre-chorus, chorus, bridge, breakdown, build,
climax, post-chorus, solo, interlude, reprise, outro — and the non-pop forms.
The platform's `classifySectionFunction` (PR-04) maps names to
`intro / verse / prechorus / chorus / bridge / breakdown / outro /
instrumental / neutral`; the following is what each function *does* to the
plan quantities the generator will be conditioned on, and what the unsupervised
segmentation of §5 can and cannot see.

| section | function in the arc | what changes in the plan | seen by unsupervised segmentation? |
|---|---|---|---|
| intro | establish key, groove, palette; thinner than the body | low density, few active tracks, unique material | yes — a thin, unique opening (`hasIntroLike`) |
| verse | state the narrative; lower energy than chorus, repeats with variation | mid density, lead-dominant, novelty low on repeats | yes — repeated letters (A, A') |
| pre-chorus / build / riser | tension rises toward the chorus/drop; harmonic rhythm often quickens | energy slope > 0, `nextSectionIntent.approach = build`, transition device | partly — a short segment before the fullest one; the *slope* is in the density arc |
| chorus / hook / refrain / drop | the peak of the form, densest, widest register, most tracks | max density, max active tracks, climax in `globalPlan` | yes — the fullest recurring section |
| post-chorus | a hook tail after the chorus, often sparser than the chorus | slight energy fall, motif carried | partly — a short section after the peak, repeated |
| bridge / middle eight | contrast: new material, often new key area, before the last chorus | novelty high, unique letter, energy varies | yes — a unique letter late in the piece |
| breakdown | the texture drops out; groove or bass alone | many tracks removed at one boundary | yes — `removedPerBoundary` spike |
| climax | the single highest point (often the last chorus, larger) | max energy; a variant (A') of the chorus with more tracks | yes — the section with peak `activeTracks`/density |
| solo / instrumental / interlude | a part leads where the vocal does not | lead role reassigned; vocal attention map `no_vocal` | partly — texture change; *which* part leads is not a bar feature here |
| reprise | a return of earlier material, often reduced | a repeat letter with lower density | yes — a repeat letter, thinner |
| outro / coda / tag | close; thin out, ritardando, or a tag repeated | low density, unique or repeated tail, ending device | yes — `hasOutroLike` |

Non-pop forms, and what "coherence" means for each:

- **Binary / ternary / rounded binary (AB, ABA, ||: A :||: BA' :||)** — the
  foundation of Baroque and Classical dance movements: coherence is the
  *return*; A' must be recognisably A. The segmentation's label rule (same
  letter ≥ 0.9, variant ≥ piece-relative threshold) is built for this.
- **Sonata form** (exposition–development–recapitulation, with first/second
  subject groups and a coda; see Caplin, *Classical Form*, 1998): coherence is
  thematic — the development *works* the exposition's motifs, the
  recapitulation restates them in the tonic. Motif recurrence across sections
  (`quotedShare`) is the relevant measure; key relationships are not modelled
  here (a known gap: `keyFromNotes` exists and could be run per section).
- **Rondo (ABACA…)** — the refrain returns between episodes; a periodic
  repeat letter with contrasting unique letters in between.
- **Theme and variations** — one section repeated as variants (A A' A'' …):
  the label rule's *variant* case is exactly this; the coherence question is
  that each variant keeps the theme's cells while the surface changes.
- **Jazz head–solos–head** — the head (a 12- or 32-bar chorus) is stated,
  repeated as improvised choruses over the same changes, then restated:
  harmonically periodic (the diagonal repeat detector fires on the changes)
  with melodically unique choruses. A generator writing a solo chorus must keep
  the *form* (the changes, the chorus length) and vary the *surface*.
- **EDM build / drop / breakdown** (Butler, *Unlocking the Groove*, 2006):
  the arc is texture-driven — layers added over a build, removed at a
  breakdown, everything in at the drop; the ensemble-evolution features
  (`addedPerBoundary`, `removedPerBoundary`, `ensemble.shape`) are the
  measurement, and the transition engine's riser/drop devices the plan.
- **Film cue arcs** — through-composed or motivic (a leitmotif recurring
  transformed), shaped to picture: coherence is motivic and dynamic, not
  sectional repetition; `meanQuotedShare` and the density arc apply, the
  repeat share does not.
- **Through-composed** (art song, much film music, some progressive rock):
  no section repeats by design; coherence is *continuity* — the seam, harmonic
  agreement, instrumentation-continuity and trajectory components apply and
  the repeat-based ones must not be read as a fault.

The last two rows are why the coherence metric of §6 reports components
separately and never folds "no repeats" into a penalty: repetition is a
property of the *form*, continuity is a property of the *writing*.

## 4. How the learned generator should consume the platform's plans

The stack already produces the plan; the design question is the interface.
Three ways, in increasing order of what they ask of the model:

1. **Context material (works with CA2 today).** Put the plan's *consequences*
   into the window as notes and controls: the harmony plan's voicings (Q-04)
   as fixed context notes, the section's density target as CA2's
   horizontal/vertical onset-density controls, the register target through
   CA2's pitch controls, the motif to restate as fixed notes in a sibling
   track. No training. This is Option D (decision report), extended to use the
   controls CA2 already has — and it is the first experiment in §7.
2. **Plan tokens (vocabulary-extended fine-tune — Option B step ii).** Add
   tokens for section function (`Section_chorus`), position in the form
   (`SectionIndex_3of7`, `SectionRepeat_2`), energy/density/tension bins,
   role, chord, and motif id, emitted as a **prefix** in the encoder input
   (MuseCoco's recipe), and train on tasks where the prefix is *true* for the
   human target. The platform's own tokenizer already lists exactly these
   fields as the Q-06 vocabulary; the extraction in §5 makes them computable
   from unlabelled scores.
3. **Memory of previous windows.** A summary of everything already written
   for this song — per track: register, density, last motif cells, the
   previous window's final bar verbatim — fed as extra encoder input
   (memory-token style, §2.4). For CA2 this is a *side model + fine-tune*; for
   ARRANGER_FM it is a design choice from day one.

In all three, the *decision* stays in the planners and the critic, which is
Wave Q's rule 1: the generator writes notes under the plan; it does not
replace the brain.

## 5. Measured — the form profile of the admitted corpus

**Code:** `artifacts/api-server/src/lib/formSegmentation.ts` (13 tests) and
`scripts/profile-form.mjs`. **Evidence:** `docs/evidence/form-profile.json`.
**Rights basis:** every analysed work is admitted by both our gate and the
authors' `no_license_conflict` subset (222,820 admitted MIDI files on disk);
the corpus was read in place.

### 5.1 Method

Per bar: a duration-weighted pitch-class histogram, a 16-bin onset-position
histogram, which (file track, channel) pairs sound, onset density, register
mean and spread. Bars are compared by cosine similarity into a self-similarity
matrix; Foote's checkerboard kernel (half-width 4 bars, Gaussian taper) gives
a novelty curve whose peaks above mean + 0.5σ, at least 4 bars apart, are
boundaries; an exact adjacent repeat (which produces no novelty) is split by
periodicity when a lag ≥ 8 bars has near-perfect self-similarity. Each segment
is compared to every earlier one along the aligned diagonal (with a length
penalty): ≥ 0.9 is the same letter, ≥ max(0.78, the piece's own 70th
percentile of bar similarity) is a variant (A'). Independently of the
boundaries, ≥ 4-bar runs of ≥ 0.92 similarity on any diagonal mark bars that
repeat somewhere. Intro/outro-like: a first/last section with a unique letter
that is thinner than the piece's median section (density < 0.7×, or fewer
active tracks) or ≤ 4 bars long. Motif recurrence: four-note skyline cells in
`buildMotifMemory`'s own key format (intervals + rhythm ratios), quoted from
earlier sections; three-note cells recur in nearly every section of nearly
every piece and were rejected as too generic for section comparison. Metre
changes are honoured in the bar grid. The thresholds were inspected on real
scores and are *not* fitted to labelled forms — none exist for PDMX.

### 5.2 Headline numbers (6,000 admitted works, ≥ 16 bars; 6,557 scanned, 0 parse failures)

- **Speed:** median 2 ms, mean 4.7 ms, max 387 ms per work (all 6,000 in
  under a minute after the rights basis is read).
- **Size:** median 33 bars (p10 17, p90 79); median 1 (file track, channel)
  pair, mean 1.8; **1,553 works (25.9 %) have ≥ 2 track/channel pairs** (a
  two-staff piano counts) and **528 (8.8 %) have ≥ 2 instrument families** —
  the same 9 % PR-53 found for arranger tasks, now on a different sample.
  3,615 works (60 %) carry at least one real time-signature change; many of
  these are a pickup bar exported as its own metre, so this overstates
  through-piece metre change.
- **Form length:** median **4 sections** per work (mean 5.3, p10 2, p90 10;
  histogram peaks at 2 and 4 sections); median section length **8 bars**
  (p10 4, p25 5, p75 9, p90 12) with the histogram's two modes at exactly 4
  and 8 bars — the phrase grid the planners already assume. Median 3
  distinct letters (p90 7).
- **Repetition:** by labels, **51.5 % of works have at least one repeated
  section**; the repeated-section share has median 0.18 and p75 0.56 (so a
  quarter of works are more than half repeats by section). By diagonals —
  which catch repeats the boundary rule merges or splits — **86 % of works
  contain a ≥ 4-bar passage that recurs**, and the median work has 92 % of
  its bars on a repeat diagonal. The two numbers bracket the truth: label
  repeats are conservative (a section must repeat *as a unit*), diagonal
  repeats are liberal (a repeated accompaniment figure counts).
- **Most common forms:** `A B` 16.6 %, `A B C` 13.1 %, `A B C D` 8.8 %,
  `A B C D E` 4.4 %, `A B C C` 3.1 %, `A A` 2.6 %, `A B B` 2.0 %, `A` 1.9 %,
  `A B C B` 1.8 %, `A B C D E F` 1.6 %, `A A B C` 1.4 %, `A A B` 1.2 %. The
  through-composed-looking `A B C …` strings are as expected for classical
  and hymn-like scores where texture stays constant across sections and
  repeats are varied rather than verbatim; the label rule then sees
  contrast where a musician would see A A' — this is the profile's main
  known bias.
- **Intro / outro:** **9.7 %** of works open with an intro-like segment,
  **9.7 %** close with an outro-like one, **1.8 %** have both.
- **Density arc:** flat 36.7 %, **arch 29.0 %**, rise 20.0 %, fall 11.9 %;
  the densest section sits at median position 0.5 of the form (mean 0.51):
  the corpus peaks in the middle, not at the end. First-section density is
  25 % below the peak on average (7.9 vs 10.4 onsets per bar).
- **Ensemble evolution** (1,498 multitrack works with ≥ 2 sections): the
  number of active tracks changes at **25.5 % of section boundaries**
  (mean 0.45 tracks added and 0.39 removed per boundary; median 0); by shape,
  **67.9 % flat**, 14.9 % rise, 6.2 % arch, 4.5 % fall. The fullest section
  is most often the first (peak position median 0). *Reading:* in this
  corpus the ensemble is mostly all in from bar one — orchestration-driven
  form (breakdowns, builds, layered entries) is a pop/EDM/film property that
  PDMX's classical share barely contains, which is a data-gap statement for
  Tier C augmentation, not a property of music.
- **Motifs:** 88 % of sections after the first share at least one four-note
  skyline cell with an earlier section (median 1.0 per work); the mean
  quoted share per section has median **0.54** (p10 0.07, p90 0.78) —
  restatement is the norm, and a generated section quoting nothing is far
  outside the human distribution.

### 5.3 What this is the basis for

- **Section-level tasks** (§7.1): the 528 multi-family works in this sample
  scale to ≈ 19–20k in the admitted corpus, each with a median of 4
  sections — on the order of 60–80k section-level examples with computed
  function/position/repeat/density/register labels, with no human labelling.
- **A form prior** for the planners: the section-count and section-length
  distributions above are what a plan should look like; a 40-section plan
  for a 3-minute song is outside them.
- **Calibration** of the coherence metric's motif and trajectory components
  (§6), which read this segmentation.

**Limits.** Unsupervised and unvalidated against labelled forms (none exist
here); over-segmentation where texture changes inside a section, under-
segmentation where a section change keeps texture, register and
instrumentation; intro/outro are thinness heuristics, not cadence or fade
detection; tracks are (file track, channel) pairs; works over 512 bars are
truncated; the sample is 6,000 of 222,820 files, deterministic, not the whole
corpus.

## 6. Measured — the whole-song coherence metric and its separation

**Code:** `artifacts/api-server/src/lib/coherenceMetric.ts` (11 tests) and
`scripts/validate-coherence-metric.mjs`. **Evidence:**
`docs/evidence/coherence-metric-live.json`.

### 6.1 What it measures

Input: per-track notes over a whole piece on a bar grid (`FormInput`, so MIDI
ticks or the platform's seconds both work via `formInputFromMidi` /
`formInputFromSecondsTracks`), the generator's window size (8 bars), and
optionally the form and the section plan the arrangement was written against.
Five components, each 0..100 or `null` when unmeasurable, weighted 0.35 / 0.2
/ 0.15 / 0.15 / 0.15 into one score:

1. **seamArtefacts** — the pasted-windows signature. Per track, five bar-to-bar
   statistics — register jump, onset-density jump, pitch-class-content jump,
   melodic leap across the bar line, and notes cut exactly at the bar line —
   each measured at the 8-bar grid lines versus at every other bar line, as a
   log₂ ratio (`seamIndex`; 0 = no seam effect). A **control grid** at the
   4-bar offset measures ordinary phrase structure the same way, and
   `excess = seamIndex − controlIndex` is what the window grid has that phrase
   structure does not. Half the mean over tracks, half the worst track: one
   pasted part is enough to break an arrangement. A statistic with fewer than
   3 seam observations is not a ratio, it is noise, and is dropped.
2. **harmonicAgreement** — per bar, the overlap between a pitched track's
   pitch-class distribution and the rest of the ensemble's; half the mean,
   half the least-agreeing track.
3. **instrumentationContinuity** — re-entries after an absence of ≥ 2 bars
   that neither a caller-supplied section boundary nor another track's
   entry/exit at that bar explains. *A form computed from the same notes may
   not explain a re-entry* — the glitch would put a boundary at itself — so
   with no caller-supplied form or plan only the ensemble can explain it.
4. **trajectorySmoothness** — roughness (mean |Δ| / σ) of the ensemble
   register and density series inside sections, plus, when a plan is given,
   Spearman rank agreement between planned and measured section density.
5. **motifRecurrence** — the mean share of each section's four-note skyline
   cells already heard in an earlier section (from `formSegmentation`).

Calibration constants map raw values to 0..100 and were set on one look at
100 human works *before* any synthetic comparison (recorded in `CALIBRATION`
with the human quantiles they came from); a monotone mapping cannot change
paired win rates.

### 6.2 Validation on real music

400 admitted multitrack works (≥ 3 tracks, ≥ 32 bars; 4,632 scanned to find
them) were scored in three conditions: **HUMAN** as written;
**TRACK_SHUFFLE** — the busiest pitched track's 8-bar windows in a random
order, notes cut at their new window's end, every other part untouched (one
pasted part; shuffled families: keys 137, ensemble 60, strings 49, reed 37,
pipe 35, brass 29, organ 17, guitar 14, bass 8, other 14); **ENSEMBLE_SHUFFLE**
— every track's windows in the same random order (good windows in no
musical order). Every condition is scored against the HUMAN work's own
section densities as the plan.

| | HUMAN | TRACK_SHUFFLE | ENSEMBLE_SHUFFLE |
|---|---|---|---|
| score, mean ± sd | **70.6 ± 13.3** | 62.5 | 55.8 |
| score, median (p10–p90) | 71.4 (53.3–88.0) | | |
| paired win rate, human > synthetic | — | **91.9 %** | **95.4 %** |
| mean difference (paired effect size) | — | 8.1 (d_paired 1.07) | 14.8 (d_paired 1.40) |
| Cohen's d (unpaired) | — | 0.62 | 1.17 |
| one-cut accuracy at the midpoint | — | 61.8 % | 72.4 % |

Per component (paired win rate, human > synthetic):

| component | TRACK_SHUFFLE | ENSEMBLE_SHUFFLE | reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| seamArtefacts | 82.6 % (Δ 9.5) | **91.1 % (Δ 32.6)** | the pasted-windows signature is real and large when every part is pasted; with one part pasted the ensemble mean is diluted and the worst-track half carries it |
| harmonicAgreement | 79.0 % | 62.0 % (Δ 0.1) | a shuffled part disagrees with its siblings; an ensemble shuffle keeps every window's inner agreement — *as designed*, and the metric says so |
| instrumentationContinuity | 57.0 % | 45.9 % | **does not separate**: shuffling windows moves entries to window lines where the ensemble also moves, and human music has plenty of unexplained returns (median 98.8, but p10 43.8) |
| trajectorySmoothness | 92.6 % | **98.1 % (Δ 22.2)** | departure from a *given* plan is detected almost always; HUMAN's plan adherence is 1.0 by construction, so this measures detection, not human virtue |
| motifRecurrence | 71.0 % (Δ 14.1) | 47.9 % | a shuffled part loses the section-wise quotation order; an ensemble shuffle keeps cells inside windows — the constructions do not simulate "never restates", which is this component's real target |

Raw seam excess (log₂): human mean **+0.10**, one-track shuffle +0.21, ensemble
shuffle **+0.75** — i.e. in a fully pasted arrangement, bar-to-bar statistics
jump on average 1.7× more at window lines than elsewhere, after subtracting
what happens at 4-bar phrase lines. Human works' worst track sits at +0.98
mean: real music *does* have one part that changes most at 8-bar lines, which
is why the worst-track term is blended, not used alone.

**Honest reading.** The metric separates human from pasted with paired win
rates above 90 % overall, driven by the seam and trajectory components; two
components (instrumentation continuity, motif recurrence under ensemble
shuffle) do not separate on these constructions and are reported as such
rather than reweighted to look better. The synthetic pieces break *order and
continuity*; they do not simulate a generator that never restates a motif or
that drops a part for no reason — those need their own constructions (a
"no-restatement" rewrite; a "random dropout" rewrite) before those components
can claim validation. All 400 works are classical/early-music scores; no
listener has rated any of them for coherence. The number here is a proxy for
the tournament, not a musical verdict.

### 6.3 Integration with the tournament (Workstream B owns the files)

Nothing in `modelTournament.ts`, `partJudge.ts` or `tournamentProviders.ts`
was edited. The intended hook, for a future *multi-window* task type:

- A multi-window task holds the whole piece's `contextTracks` (already notes
  in absolute seconds) and a sequence of windows; a provider returns the
  held-out part over all of them.
- Build the input with `formInputFromSecondsTracks(tracks, barStartsSeconds,
  end)` where `tracks` = context tracks + the candidate part as one more
  track, and `barStartsSeconds` from the task's tempo/metre (the tournament
  already computes `barSeconds`); call `measureCoherence(input, { windowBars,
  form: humanForm, plan })` with the human work's `segmentForm` result as the
  form and its section densities as the plan, exactly as the validation
  script does.
- Report `score`, `components.seamArtefacts.worstTrack` (should be the
  candidate part when it is the pasted one) and the human anchor's score on
  the same piece; the judge's per-window score and this whole-piece score are
  two columns, not one number.

## 7. Recommendation

**In three sentences.** Keep the platform's rule-derived global-to-local plan
as the brain and make the learned generator *consume* it — first through the
controls Composer's Assistant 2 already has (density, register, fixed context
notes for harmony and motifs), then through plan-prefix tokens in the
vocabulary-extended fine-tune the decision report already schedules — while a
whole-song memory (a per-track summary of what has been written so far) is
added as extra encoder input in that same fine-tune. Generate window by window
under that plan and memory, judge every candidate on the tournament's window
score **and** on the whole-song coherence score of §6 with the human work as
the anchor, so a pasted-windows output can never win on windows alone. Do not
extend `MAX_LEN` as the route to coherence, and do not build a from-scratch
hierarchical model before the fine-tune has shown whether a section-aware CA2
transfers — the form profile of §5 says the training data for section-level
tasks exists, and the metric of §6 says the failure it must avoid is
measurable.

### 7.1 The section-level task (training and evaluation)

A **section-level task** is an arranger task whose window is a *section*, not
8 bars, and whose context is the *whole piece minus the target section's part*
plus a plan prefix — so the model learns to write a section that belongs to
its song:

- **Source:** an admitted PDMX work with ≥ 2 families and ≥ 2 sections from
  `segmentForm` (the profile in §5 says 8.8 % of admitted works have ≥ 2
  families, ≈ 19–20k works; nearly all have ≥ 2 sections).
- **Target:** one family's notes over one section `[startBar, endBar)`
  (median 8 bars, p90 12 in the profile) — the human's writing.
- **Context, in the window:** every other family over the same section
  (as today's tasks).
- **Context, outside the window (new):** a *song memory* — for every family:
  active/inactive per earlier section, register mean, density, and the last
  bar verbatim before the section; plus the target family's own material in
  the *earlier* sections of the same letter (if the section is `A'`, the
  model sees how `A` went) — that is how restatement is learned from data
  rather than hoped for.
- **Plan prefix (new tokens):** `Section_<function|letter>`,
  `SectionIndex_<i>_of_<n>`, `SectionRepeat_<k>` (k-th time this letter),
  `SectionKind_<intro|body|outro>`, `Density_<bin>` and `Register_<bin>` of the
  human target, `Ensemble_<activeTracks>`, `Arc_<rise|arch|fall|flat>`. Every
  value is *computed from the human score* by `formSegmentation`, so the
  prefix is always true for the target — the MuseCoco discipline.
- **Evaluation:** (i) the tournament's per-window judge on the section;
  (ii) the whole-song coherence score with the generated section spliced
  into the human piece, against the human anchor — `worstTrack` must not be
  the generated part; (iii) plan adherence: did the density land in the bin
  the prefix asked for. All three per seed, N seeds, as PR-58 requires.
- **Splits:** work-level, as PR-53; near-duplicate detection is still an open
  item and matters more here (a repeated section *inside* a work is the point,
  a duplicate work across splits is a leak).

### 7.2 The first experiment

**"Does a section-aware CA2 stop pasting?"** — before any training:

1. Build 200 section-level tasks from the profile's multi-family works
   (script: extend `extract-arranger-tasks.mjs` to use `segmentForm`
   sections as windows; this PR does not do it).
2. Run CA2+CTX **three ways** on each: (a) as today, 8-bar windows in
   sequence, no memory — the pasted baseline; (b) 8-bar windows with the
   *previous window's output* as fixed context notes and CA2's density /
   pitch controls set from the section plan (Option D extended, zero
   training); (c) the section as one window where it fits `MAX_LEN`, else (b).
3. Score all with the window judge and with `measureCoherence` against the
   human anchor. The hypothesis is that (b) closes a large part of the
   seam-artefact gap at zero training cost; the residual gap is the budget
   case for the fine-tune with plan tokens and memory (Option B step ii),
   measured, not estimated.
4. Only if (b)/(c) leave the coherence gap mostly open does the
   from-scratch, Museformer/NotaGen-style hierarchical `ARRANGER_FM` move up
   the list — and by then the section tasks, the metric and the anchor exist.

### 7.3 What this study does not settle

- No listener has rated coherence; the metric is validated against synthetic
  damage, not against human judgement (Gate C/D remain the arbiters).
- The corpus is classical/early-music; pop verse–chorus behaviour (post-chorus,
  build, drop) is described from the literature and the platform's own
  planners, not measured here.
- Key relationships across sections (sonata, modulating bridges) are not in
  the feature set; `keyFromNotes` per section is the obvious next feature.
- `MAX_LEN` cost was reasoned from T5's relative-position buckets, not
  measured on CA2; the number is a claim about the architecture, not a run.
