## 0. Two findings that change how every number below should be read

**F1 — Several "musical" critic dimensions never look at the arrangement.** `musicCritic.ts` (PR-11, the critic that runs inside the orchestrator and therefore inside every production generation) grades `harmony` from the *Song Model's own* chords and melody (`musicCritic.ts:148-198`: inputs are `songModel` and its musical map only), `contrast` from the Song Model's style fingerprint and vocal register map (`musicCritic.ts:419-430`), and `motifCoherence` from the *source melody's* motifs (`musicCritic.ts:395-417`). None of the three takes `plan` or `trackModels`. Consequently the benchmark's `harmonyScore` (defined as the selected candidate's `harmony` dimension, `arrangementBenchmark.ts:74-78`) is invariant to the composer: it is 58.33 for `REFERENCE_PART_COMPOSER` and 58.33 for `CONTEXT_AWARE_ARRANGER` (`docs/evidence/context-aware-vs-reference-benchmark.json`, aggregate block), and unchanged by the PR-33 performance fix (`docs/evidence/benchmark-playability-drift.json` before/after). The master plan calls `harmonyScore` 58 "the most honest number here and the clearest target" (`docs/master-plan.md`, "Benchmark baseline" section) — it is a property of the nine synthetic Song Models, not of any arrangement. Five more dimensions (`leadCompatibility`, `orchestration`, `sectionDevelopment`, `transitions`, `performancePotential`; `musicCritic.ts:288-393, 432-452, 477-494`) read only the plan. Note-level evidence enters only through the hard-rule gate (`:120-139`), groove kick/bass lock (`:233-244`), voice leading (`:249-286`) and playability (`:454-475`).

**F2 — The in-orchestrator critic→repair loop changes the score but not the music.** `runCriticRepairLoop` (`criticRepairLoop.ts:214-277`) clones the plan (`applyPlanRepairs`, `:109`), edits plan fields (adds a colour layer, a transition device, ducks density multipliers, adds register resolutions, shifts section energies ±0.08; `:113-205`), and re-critiques. Its result type carries `passes` and `finalCritique` but **no plan** (`lib/db/src/schema/music-studio.ts:3610-3619`). The orchestrator uses only `repair.finalCritique` (`arrangementOrchestrator.ts:429-430`); the composed `trackModels` from `:412-414` go unchanged into performance (`:433`) and the original `plan` into everything else. So a candidate can gain up to ~30 critic points (band 50→80, `criticRepairLoop.ts:30-31`) from repairs that were never applied, and that inflated score is what ranks it (`finalScore`, `:559-575, 616-619`) and what the provider reports as `symbolicScore` (`arrangementOrchestratorProvider.ts:213-216, 270`). The live e2e summary "symbolic 75/100 · 1 repair pass(es)" (`docs/evidence/orchestrator-live-e2e.json`) is this. Dimensions that "genuinely need note regeneration are left for the injected applier" (`criticRepairLoop.ts:200-203`) and nothing is injected in production.

Both findings mean the baseline table (criticScore 74.56, harmonyScore 58.33, sectionConsistency 100, candidateDiversity 50.4) is not a measure of arrangement quality that a redesign can be compared against without first fixing the measure.

## 1. BENCHMARK

### 1.1 Is there a frozen corpus? What is in it?

Yes, one — synthetic. `benchmarkCorpus.ts:43-107` (`BENCHMARK_CORPUS`, PR-18) holds 9 cases synthesised from specifications (`:1-12`: "synthesised from explicit musical specifications … rather than transcribed from recordings"). `buildBenchmarkSongModel` (`:112+`) cycles a 4-chord progression two bars per chord (`:144`) and a 4-note `melodyFigure` per case.

| id | genre | input | bpm | meter | key | bars | stems |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pop-full | pop | full_song | 120 | 4/4 | C | 40 | vocals, drums, bass, keys |
| ballad-piano-vocal | ballad | piano_vocal | 68 | 4/4 | F | 28 | vocals, keys |
| rock-full | rock | full_song | 148 | 4/4 | E | 36 | vocals, drums, bass, guitar |
| dance-full | dance | full_song | 126 | 4/4 | Am | 40 | vocals, drums, bass, synth |
| acoustic-demo | acoustic | rough_demo | 92 | 4/4 | G | 32 | vocals, guitar |
| orchestral-midi | orchestral | midi | 76 | 3/4 | D | 36 | strings, brass, keys |

| jazz-full | jazz | full_song | 132 | 4/4 | Bb | 48 | vocals, drums, bass, keys |
| cinematic-midi | cinematic | midi | 60 | 4/4 | Cm | 32 | strings, brass, keys, percussion |

Seven of nine are 4/4; tempi 60–148; lengths 24–48 bars (≈1–2 minutes); every progression is a diatonic 4-chord loop; no swing, no key change, no non-western idiom beyond the label "ethnic" with a 7/8 meter. **No human material of any kind is in it.**

The real corpus is a contract with zero entries: `benchmarkCorpusPlan.ts:236` `export const REAL_BENCHMARK_CORPUS: CorpusEntry[] = [];` with targets 100 songs / 30 human-gold (`:99-100`), a coverage report (`corpusCoverage`, `:184-220`; required spread `:87-97` incl. 3/4 and 6/8, swung, non_western, dense, large ensembles), and `humanGold?` per entry (`:79-83`). `realCorpusBenchmark.ts:121-138` refuses the north-star level: "No song in the corpus has a human gold arrangement … cannot be run." The master plan status table says "corpus empty (0 of 100 songs)" (`docs/master-plan.md:31`).

Human-origin symbolic music *does* exist in the repo's evaluation machinery, but outside this benchmark: PDMX `no_license_conflict` (222,856 admitted works, `docs/evidence/pdmx-ingest-summary.json`), used by the tournament (12 classical tasks × 3 seeds × 5 arms, `docs/evidence/model-tournament-live.json` `sampling`), the judge calibration (30,570 human 8-bar windows), the coherence metric (400 works) and reward-model pairs (6,000 works). These are human *parts* used as anchors for 8–24-bar part-infill tasks — not human gold *arrangements* of a benchmark song. `PROFESSIONAL_HUMAN_GOLD` is named as the final bar in `docs/model-discovery/decision-pack.md` §10b and in `master-plan.md:2295` ("still to be sourced"); no code or data carries one.

### 1.2 Can a change be compared against a baseline today?

Partly, and only against the synthetic corpus:

- Command: `pnpm --filter @workspace/api-server run benchmark [-- --render] [-- --json]` (`artifacts/api-server/package.json:12`, `scripts/run-benchmark.mjs`, `src/benchmark-cli.ts:13-22`). It prints a table or JSON; **it does not compare** — `compareBenchmarkRuns` (`arrangementBenchmark.ts:211-248`) has no CLI entry; you must save two `--json` runs and call it from code.
- Metrics: `criticScore, audioScore, playabilityErrors, harmonyScore, sectionConsistency, candidateDiversity, renderFailures, noteCount, latencyMs` (`:21-24`). Verdict rule: improved on ≥1 quality metric, regressed on none, tolerance 1.0 absolute (`:216-238`); latency and noteCount excluded from "quality".
- Stored results: **no stored baseline `BenchmarkRun` JSON exists.** The baseline lives as a hand-typed table in `docs/master-plan.md` ("Benchmark baseline — the number every later change is judged against") and as `aggregateBefore/After` in `docs/evidence/benchmark-playability-drift.json`. The only in-repo automated comparison run is `docs/evidence/context-aware-vs-reference-benchmark.json` (via `contextAwareBenchmark.ts:79-91`). `arrangerTrainingPipeline.ts:184-188` also uses `compareBenchmarkRuns` as the promotion gate for `YOUR_ARRANGER_MODEL`.
- Determinism: the orchestrator is fixed at `now = new Date(0)` in production (`arrangementOrchestratorProvider.ts:196`) and the reference composer is deterministic, so runs are reproducible; `runId` is a hash of corpus ids + system name (`arrangementBenchmark.ts:173-175`), not of the code version.

Three of the metrics are known non-discriminating and the plan says so (`master-plan.md` baseline section): `sectionConsistency` is always 100 (it counts plan novelty values in [0.1, 0.9], `arrangementBenchmark.ts:55-64`), `candidateDiversity` is a note-count spread (`:66-72`) that reads 50.4 on every run, `audioScore` 87–93 everywhere. Add F1: `harmonyScore` is composer-invariant. That leaves `criticScore` (inflated per F2) and `playabilityErrors` as the only moving parts — and `playabilityErrors` is the one metric with real teeth (see §2).

### 1.3 What past evidence files actually measured (representative, with honest limits)

1. **`benchmark-playability-drift.json`** — 9 synthetic cases; `playabilityErrors` 4.33 → 0 after `performanceEngine.clampPolyphony`. Root cause documented: performance lengthening bowed strings past the legato tolerance on the two MIDI cases (26 and 13 errors). Limit: every other aggregate identical before/after (criticScore 74.56, harmonyScore 58.33) — the benchmark saw a physics defect and nothing else.
2. **`context-aware-vs-reference-benchmark.json` + `context-aware-regression-fixed.json`** — the only two-arm comparison ever run: criticScore 74.56 → 74.0 ("unchanged" within tolerance 1), playabilityErrors 0 → 6.89 (one case 0 → 62), `do_not_promote`. The follow-up diagnosed the 62 as `impossible_leap` on bass caused by a hi-hat at MIDI 42 being treated as a unison and a `\bdrum\b` regex not matching "drums". Limit: the critic could not tell the two composers apart at all; the file says "the critic score is the pipeline grading itself".
3. **`human-blind-ratings-live.json`** — one rater (the owner), 49 of 50 tournament pairs, five comparisons at n = 9–10: HUMAN vs CA2+CTX 7–3 (p 0.34), CA2+CTX vs REFERENCE 4–6, CA2 vs CA2+CTX 5–5, **HUMAN vs REFERENCE 5–5**, CONTEXT_AWARE vs CA2+CTX 3–6. Limit stated in the file and decision pack §1: not one comparison distinguishable from a coin flip; the experiment "has not demonstrated sensitivity".
4. **`listening-benchmark-v2-{report,live,sensitivity}.json`** — 21 longer passages (16-bar and complete 8–24-bar sections) from the same 12 classical PDMX works; 126 positive-control pairs; a 50-pair session opened, **0 votes**. The recorded *proxy* side is the closest thing to a positive-control table for automated metrics: judge 1.1 prefers the human on 21/21 at pitch_shift 30 % and 60 %, note_deletion 50 %, random_pitch 30 %; only 12/21 at pitch_shift 10 % and 11/21 at onset_jitter 30 %; coherence metric 18/21 at pitch 60 %, 13–14/21 on the weak rungs (`listening-benchmark-v2-sensitivity.json` → `proxy.perComparison`). Limit: classical only; nobody has listened.
5. **`judge-calibration.json`** — judge 1.1 over 30,570 human 8-bar windows from 2,000 works: windows with ≥1 playability error 1.6 % (was 26.6 % under 1.0); 564 `ambiguous_case`; `unrealistic_repetition` fires on 68–89 % of human windows (decision pack §4) and is therefore not a gate. Limit: the physics reference is compiled from orchestration texts, not measured; only the physical half of the judge is calibrated.
6. **`coherence-metric-live.json`** — 400 human PDMX works vs the same works with one track's 8-bar windows shuffled / all tracks shuffled: paired win rate 0.919 / 0.954, Cohen's d 0.62 / 1.17; `instrumentationContinuity` does not separate (0.57). Limit: validated against synthetic damage, not listeners; not used in production.
7. **`music-reward-model-v0.json`** — 212,090 synthetic preference pairs; in-distribution accuracy 0.963, **held-out corruption families 0.658** with two families below chance (0.38, 0.23), agreement with the owner's 50 votes 24/50; `rewardModelGate() → FAIL`. This is the "reward-model trap" made measurable (decision pack §10b).
8. **`orchestrator-live-e2e.json` / `definition-of-done-e2e.json`** — production runs through `POST /arrangements/{id}/generate provider=ARRANGEMENT_ORCHESTRATOR`: 3 candidates requested, 2 `diversity_rejected` at distance 0.048 (threshold 0.25), job `errorCode: INSUFFICIENT_DIVERSITY` on a *succeeded* job; every candidate "symbolic 75/100 · 0 playability error(s) · 1 repair pass(es)". Limit: the Song Model was the synthetic `pop-full` case seeded into the project (file's own note).
9. **`listening-room-live.json` / `tournament-listening-live.json`** — Gate C mechanics: one pair per candidate session, owner votes recorded and excluded, six dev-minted identities as "independent raters" on one pair; a 10-pair smoke session rated by one second identity (11 votes). These prove routes and blindness, not preference.

### 3.3 What the gates require, and the standing rule

- Gate C: ≥ 5 independent raters, ≥ 60 % release share, owner excluded (`blindListening.ts:24-26, 154`).
- Sensitivity gate (V2): as above; a session with no control pairs is `insufficient_data` by construction (`listeningSensitivity.ts:288-292`) — the PR-71 session is therefore declared unreadable, correctly.
- Gate D (Q-15): 30–50 songs, ≥ 10 musicians, one renderer/loudness, owner does not vote, ten scored aspects (`master-plan.md:5843`) — **plan only; no code** (the term appears nowhere in `src/`).
- Reward-model gate: held-out-family accuracy ≥ 0.8, monotone calibration, Human > every AI arm (`master-plan.md` PR-77 entry) — failed.
- Pairwise-critic gate: must beat the critic-only baseline on a held-out split (`pairwiseCritic.ts` header) — never trained (2 events).

**Compliance with the standing rule** ("evaluators may gate decisions only after positive controls; a null result shows the evaluator lacks sensitivity"): the V2 benchmark, the reward model and the judge calibration follow it explicitly. Gate C as built does **not** carry a positive control: a candidate session is one pair, two brain candidates, no degraded arm, so a 5-rater 60 % verdict could be reached by a room that cannot hear a 60 % pitch shift. By the repo's own rule Gate C is not yet allowed to promote anything, independent of rater count. Likewise the production ranking (`candidateQuality` + perceptual PCM, §2) gates selection today without any demonstrated sensitivity.

## 5. REPAIR & BACKTRACKING

**`criticRepairLoop.ts`** — what it can change: plan fields only, via the default applier (`:109-208`): section `activeInstrumentFamilies` + a new `roleAssignment` (sectionDevelopment), transition `devices` (transitions), budget `densityMultiplier → 0.6` under the vocal (leadCompatibility), `registerOccupancy.resolutions` (orchestration), section-target `energy ±0.08` (contrast). Bounds: ≤ 3 passes (`:26`), only when 50 ≤ score < 80 with fixable hard errors (`:35-54`), stop when gain < 1.5 (`:260`). Diagnoses the originating layer? No — repairs are keyed by critic dimension (`:64-76`), never by "planner vs composer vs performance". Reopens a plan decision after a downstream finding? It edits plan decisions, but the edited plan is thrown away (F2), so effectively no. Untouched material preserved? Trivially — nothing is touched.

**`playabilityRepair.ts`** — note-level, deterministic, bounded (3 leap passes, 8 polyphony passes, `residual` reported `:166`); may **drop notes** (`report.dropped`). Applied to every performed track in production (`arrangementOrchestrator.ts:464-474`); counts are put in the `perform` stage evidence (`:589-603`) but the provider persists only `"stage:status"` strings (`arrangementOrchestratorProvider.ts:228, 277`), so the number of notes folded/dropped in a shipped candidate is **not stored**.

**`candidateRepair.ts` / bounded repair** — layers it may change: notes, cc, articulations, automation and `appliedDirectives` inside `[startBar, endBar]` on `affectedTrackIds` (`:210-249, 601-637`); plan `sections[].tracks/trackDirectives/activeTracks` for affected tracks (`:574-599`); hierarchy semantic fields only (`function, development, targetEnergy, targetDensity, intent, vocalSpace`, `:450-494`); composition-intelligence phrases/motifs in scope (`:542-569`). Bounded by: the finding's `permissibleRepairOperations` (`:204-249`, six operations, schema `music-studio.ts:1881-1887`), canonical hierarchy ownership/coordinates immutable (`:382-436`), motif lineage identity preserved (`:513-541`), `MAX_REPAIR_ATTEMPTS = 2` (`:16`), seed source-owned (`:14`). Untouched material preserved and **verified**: `outsideScopePreserved` byte-equality over notes/cc/articulations/automation/directives/hierarchy/plan sections (`:735-776`); a violation raises `RepairScopeViolationError` → `evaluation.status = "repair_scope_violated"` (`arrangementGeneration.ts:1394-1397, 1721`). Does it diagnose the originating layer? No: `musicalReason` is the critic's sentence; the proposal is whatever the same provider writes again, merged in scope. Does it reopen a plan decision? Only inside the scope's hierarchy semantic fields; the global/section plan is not re-derived. Improvement is judged on `mean(musicCritic, audioCritic)` vs the source (`:1598-1607`) and appended to the producer ledger as `repair_completed` improved/rejected (`:1870-1890`).

**Scoped regeneration (U5)** — `scopedRegeneration.ts:376-410`: whole-song re-orchestration (≥ 3 candidates), merged over allowed scopes with lock verification, ranked on the *merged* result. It is the only path that re-runs the planner, and it is driven by a brief, not by a finding. Its own warning text admits the deterministic composer "writes the same part again" unless the brief changes (`:408`).

**Backtracking:** there is no mechanism that takes a critique or audio finding and reopens an upstream decision (instrumentation, register, density target, harmony plan). Every repair is forward-only and either plan-cosmetic (F2), physics-only (playability), or "rewrite the same bars with the same seed and keep them if the score went up".

## 6. FAILURE TAXONOMY & TELEMETRY

**Explicit codes that exist**

- Job level (`music_generation_jobs.status/stage/error/errorCode/retryable/attempt`, `lib/db/src/schema/music-studio.ts:1525+`): `CANDIDATE_EVALUATION_FAILED`, `INSUFFICIENT_DIVERSITY` (on a *succeeded* job), `CANCELLED`, `PROVIDER_CANCELLATION_UNCONFIRMED`, `PROVIDER_CANCELLATION_PENDING`, `PROVIDER_RUNTIME_UNAVAILABLE`, `PROVIDER_INCOMPLETE_RESULT`, `PROVIDER_EXECUTION_FAILED`, `RETRIES_EXHAUSTED` (`arrangementGeneration.ts:1846-1856, 1960-1975, 2143, 2161, 2304`). Stages: `preparing_inputs, running_model, provider_runtime_unavailable, rendering_candidates, analyzing_candidates, evaluation_failed, insufficient_diversity, complete, retry_queued, failed, cancelled, cancellation_pending`.
- Candidate level: `status` `validated | rejected | diversity_rejected | repair_not_improved | selected`; `evaluation.status` enum `plan_received, rendering, render_succeeded, analyzing, evaluated, render_failed, analysis_failed, diversity_rejected, repair_not_improved, repair_scope_violated` (`music-studio.ts:2047-2057`); `evaluation.error` free text (`arrangementGeneration.ts:1730`).
- Constraint engine: `code: string` (not an enum, `musicalConstraints.ts:31`); observed codes `out_of_range, excess_polyphony, impossible_leap, breath_violation, unrealistic_repetition, impossible_hat_state` plus `leap`/`leaps` variants; severity error/warning; `suggestedFix` text.
- Critic findings: symbolic ids `music-critic-v2:<dimension>:<section>:<start>-<end>:<trackIds>` (`candidateQuality.ts:891-897`) with `evidenceReferences` and `permissibleRepairOperations`; audio ids `possible-clipping-<ms>`, `abrupt-transition-<ms>`, `repetitive-texture-<ms>`, `low-dynamics-<ms>`, `vocal-support-<ms>`, `track-energy-dominance-0` (`perceptualAudioCritic.ts:91-166`).
- Judge-error taxonomy (calibration only): `VIOLATION_CLASSES` (`judgeCalibration.ts:40-51`) — nine classes incl. `judge_false_positive`, `instrument_mapping_error`, `ambiguous_case`, with gate thresholds (`:623-632`).
- Orchestrator: `StageRecord {stage, status ok|skipped|failed, detail, evidence}` for 11 stages (`arrangementOrchestrator.ts:55-65`, `traceable = stages.length === 11` `:645`).

**What is missing:** no unified failure taxonomy across layers (plan / compose / perform / render / mix / export); no code says *which layer* caused a finding; `INSUFFICIENT_DIVERSITY` conflates "strategies too similar" with success.

**Can one answer, from stored data…**

- *"Which critic objected?"* — for the job-runner critics, yes: `evaluation.musicCritic.dimensions[*].findings` and `evaluation.audioCritic.dimensions[*].findings` are persisted per candidate (`publicCandidateEvaluation`, `candidateRanking.ts:18+`). For the in-orchestrator critic and its repair loop, **no**: only `parameters.symbolicScore`, `repairPasses` (a count), `stages` (`"plan:ok,…,render:skipped,…"`) and a one-line `summary` with the top weakness survive (`arrangementOrchestratorProvider.ts:228, 259-280`); `CriticRepairPass.requests/applied/scoreBefore/After` are dropped.
- *"What repair occurred?"* — bounded repair: yes (`evaluation.repair`: `sourceCandidateId, findingId, scope, musicalReason, changedScopes, sourceQualityScore, repairedQualityScore, improved`; `music-studio.ts:3800-3820`) plus a `repair_completed` ledger row. Playability repair in production: **no** (counts not persisted). Critic-loop repair: only the pass count.
- *"What changed between iteration N and N+1?"* — both candidate rows keep full `trackModels` and `evaluatedPlan`, and `changedScopes` lists touched hierarchy ids, so a diff is *computable* offline; no stored diff, no per-note provenance of "repaired here". Evidence files under `docs/evidence/` are hand-shaped per PR with no shared schema; cross-PR comparison is possible only for the benchmark aggregates and the tournament/judge JSON, which do share shapes.

## 7. Minimum evaluation framework before large changes (≤ 1.5 pages)

The redesign should not start from a new evaluation stack; it should freeze what already discriminates, fix the two integrity defects, and add the smallest set of controls that makes the rest trustworthy.

**7.1 Fix the measure first (zero new modules)**
1. In `arrangementBenchmark.ts`, replace `harmonyScore` with a candidate-dependent harmony metric: reuse `partJudge.chordToneShare` and `contextClashShare` (`partJudge.ts:160-174, 220-240`) computed on the selected candidate's non-percussion tracks against the Song Model chords. Keep the old number as `sourceHarmonyScore` so the baseline remains readable.
2. Report `criticScore` twice: `initialCritique.overallScore` (the critic of what was actually written; the orchestrator already computes it and discards it, `arrangementOrchestrator.ts:428, 577`) and `finalCritique` (after the unapplied loop). Rank on the former until the loop applies its plan or is removed. Persist `CriticRepairPass[]` in `candidate.parameters` (they are small).
3. Replace `sectionConsistency` and `candidateDiversity` with `coherenceMetric` components (`trajectorySmoothness`, `seamArtefacts`, `motifRecurrence`; `coherenceMetric.ts`) and `candidateDistance` from `candidateDiversity.ts:116-135` averaged over candidate pairs. Both exist and the former has a positive control.

**7.2 Frozen corpus composition (reuse `benchmarkCorpusPlan.ts`)**
- Tier S — keep the 9 synthetic cases as a regression floor (byte-reproducible; they caught three real physics defects). Never report them as quality.
- Tier H — 30–40 rights-cleared human works as `CorpusEntry` rows in `REAL_BENCHMARK_CORPUS`, drawn from PDMX `no_license_conflict` multitrack works the tournament and coherence study already admit (`docs/evidence/pdmx-ingest-summary.json`, `corpus-profile.json`). Use `corpusCoverage` (`benchmarkCorpusPlan.ts:184-220`) to force ≥ 3 per required value: 3/4 and 6/8, swung, dense, large ensemble, non_western; where PDMX cannot supply a value (produced pop/dance/Mizrahi — it cannot, per PR-76/77 limits), record the gap in `gaps[]` rather than filling it synthetically. For each work the *benchmark task* is: strip N families → arrange → compare against the human original as `vsHumanGold` at the *part* level (this is exactly the tournament task shape, `tournamentTask.ts`, not verified in depth). That gives a human anchor on day one without a commissioned "gold arrangement"; the full-song `PROFESSIONAL_HUMAN_GOLD` tier stays a separate, later target and its `levelBlocker` (`realCorpusBenchmark.ts:121-130`) stays honest.
- Tier P — 3–5 of the owner's own produced songs (rights `owned_by_operator`) with confirmed Song Models (PR-98's `trusted_automatically` path), as the only produced-genre anchor. Reported per-song, never aggregated with Tier H.

**7.3 Baseline snapshot procedure**
- Add `-- --out <file>` and `-- --compare <baseline.json>` to `benchmark-cli.ts` so `compareBenchmarkRuns` runs from the CLI; store `docs/evidence/benchmark-baseline/<gitSha>.json` (full `BenchmarkRun`, both tiers, `render: true` for Tier S only). Extend `runId` with the git sha (`train-reward-model-v0.mjs` already records `gitSha`).
- Snapshot once before the redesign branch, with the §7.1 fixes applied, and pin `MUSIC_CRITIC_VERSION`, `AUDIO_CRITIC_VERSION`, `PART_JUDGE_VERSION`, `COHERENCE_METRIC_VERSION` in the file. A change to any metric version invalidates comparison and requires re-running the baseline sha.

**7.4 Metric set with positive controls (reuse `listeningDegradations.ts` + `symbolicCorruptions.ts`)**
Run the six V2 degradation rungs and the 19 `symbolicCorruptions` families over every Tier H anchor, through `evaluateCandidateMusicalFit`, `evaluateRenderedPcm` (after the production render path), `critiqueArrangement`, `partJudge`, and `coherenceMetric`. Record per metric per family the detection rate with `exactBinomialCi` (`listeningSensitivity.ts:80-96`). Rules, mirroring the sensitivity gate: a metric may **gate** only families it detects at ≥ 90 % at the strong rung; it may **inform** where it is above chance; a metric that detects nothing is reported and demoted (as `unrealistic_repetition` already is). Already-known from the V2 proxy: judge chord-tone/coverage/collapse gate pitch and deletion damage; nothing automated hears onset_jitter 30 % — so timing/groove needs a new metric or stays human-only. This table is the missing positive-control ledger for §2 and costs no GPU (the V2 report ran 126 pairs in ~4 minutes, `listening-benchmark-v2-report.json` ranAt/finishedAt).

**7.5 Human A/B protocol (reuse, do not rebuild)**
- Every Gate C candidate session must carry control pairs: extend `buildListeningPairs` (`blindListening.ts:76-93`) to add 2–3 `HUMAN_DEGRADED` pairs per session using `degradeSideMidi`, and have `sessionResults` refuse a Gate C verdict when `sensitivityReport(...).gate.verdict !== "may_judge_training"`. The report function already accepts any session (`listeningSensitivity.ts:229-234`).
- One renderer, one loudness on both sides: `LISTENING_SYNTH_V2` via `tournamentAudio`'s `renderer` option (PR-72) — not the candidate's evaluation render vs another's.
- Before/after the redesign: a 50-pair V2-style session per Tier (H and P) with `human_vs_<system>` and `<old>_vs_<new>` comparisons drawn by `selectTournamentPairs` quotas (`tournamentListening.ts:96-181`), rated first by the owner (sensitivity + direction, not verdict), then by ≥ 5 independent raters for Gate C on the `<old>_vs_<new>` pairs only. Owner votes stay excluded from verdicts; `preferenceRecords` feed the reward model only after the session's gate passes.
- Keep the decision table discipline: name no cause a control has not isolated (`listeningSensitivity.ts:178-227`).

**7.6 Telemetry minimum**
Persist the orchestrator `StageRecord.evidence` (not just `stage:status`), the playability-repair counts per track, and `CriticRepairPass[]` on the candidate; add `originLayer: plan | compose | perform | render | mix` to `CriticRepairFinding` and `CandidateAudioCriticFinding` (nullable, filled where derivable — e.g. a playability violation appearing only in `performedConstraints` is `perform`; `nativeRenderGate` reasons are `render`). Without these, the redesign's own repair loop cannot be audited any better than today's.

**Order:** 7.1 → 7.3 (snapshot) → 7.4 (control ledger) → 7.2 Tier H ingestion → 7.5. Nothing in this list trains a model or needs a GPU; every module named already exists and has tests.
